          How this works and what it leaves out
          Market
          A Statistics Canada census division: a county, regional municipality or equivalent. Unnamed divisions take the name of their largest town.
          New residents and people leaving
          Statistics Canada publishes only net moves within Canada at this level, so both figures are estimates and undercount real movement. New residents are immigrants plus any net gain from domestic moves and temporary residents. People leaving are net emigration plus any net loss from those two.
          Status
          High turnover: at least — new residents and — people leaving per 1,000. Growing: net gain of at least — per 1,000. Shrinking: net loss. Steady: everything else.
          Main patient group
          The age group most over-represented compared with Canada. Young families: ages 0–14 and 30–44. Young adults: 15–29. Retirees: 65 and over. Ages 45–64 count as older professionals where household income or the share with a university degree is at or above the Canadian figure, and as older working-age otherwise.
          Treatment suggestions
          These are general marketing starting points, not clinical guidance or Statistics Canada data. Check your provincial dental regulator's advertising rules before using testimonials, comparisons or discounts.
          Left out
          Age groups are a stand-in for household type, so a 35-year-old without children counts toward young families. Dental offices count businesses with employees, not dentists, and leave out solo practices with no staff. Current postal-area movement is not published for free. Move-out counts by postal area are not published at all.
